Here is the exact UI Design Language Brief you should copy-paste to your app-building agent.

Do not describe screens to them. Describe the system that generates the UI. Tell them this:

---

"Build the UI using this exact visual engine and design system:"

1. The Core Foundation (Material You with a Cyber Twist)

· Base Theme: Enforce a Pure Dark palette (#000000 background) with a secondary layer tint of #121212 for surfaces.
· Primary Accent: Use Neon/Volt Green (#00E676). This is the only color that grabs attention—it is used exclusively for active states, key data points, and primary CTAs.
· Typography: Use Inter or Roboto with extreme weight contrast. Headline numbers (like 2h 28m) are weight 900 with wide letter-spacing, while subtitles are weight 400 with 50% opacity to create instant visual hierarchy.

2. The "3D Premium" Illusion (Depth Architecture)

Do not use actual 3D rendering. Create the premium illusion through Multi-Layer Elevation:

· All container cards (like the Top Used Apps or 21-day chart boxes) must use elevation with two distinct shadows—a tight, dark ambient shadow and a wider, soft translucent shadow—to make them float aggressively off the screen.
· Apply a subtle linear gradient overlay (top-to-bottom, from 20% accent color to 0% transparent) on the top edges of these floating cards to simulate a "glow" catching the light, giving a high-end, metallic hardware feel.

3. The "Glow" Element (Vital for Premium Feel)

· Any active toggle, the "Save" button, or selected segmented control must have a soft outer glow (using dropShadow or blur behind the view) rather than a standard flat color. This makes the UI look reactive and OLED-optimized.

4. Component Architecture (Building Blocks)

Implement these specific widgets universally across the app:

· Floating Segmented Controls: Instead of tabs, use pill-shaped dividers (like the 15s/30s/60s) where the selected option is a filled neon circle with white text, and unselected options are just text with 60% opacity.
· The "Glass" Toggles: The permission toggles (Notifications, Usage Stats) should be large, custom Switch components with a translucent track (#FFFFFF at 15% opacity) and a neon-filled thumb that casts a tiny glow underneath.
· Spacer-Heavy Layouts: Maintain extreme negative space. Every card must have a minimum internal padding of 24dp, and elements inside must be separated by thick, invisible gutters to mimic high-end dashboard software.

5. The Data Visualization Style

For the 21-day chart:

· Use Cylindrical 3D bars (achieved by drawing a normal bar with a slightly darker slanted cap on top).
· The bar color must be a gradient from Neon Green to a darker teal, with a sharp drop-shadow beneath each bar to make them look like physical pillars rising from the X-axis.

6. Micro-interactions (Non-Negotiable for "Modern")

· All card expansions, screen transitions, and toggle switches must trigger a Staggered Fade-in with a spring damping effect.
· When a user interacts with a button (like Save Delay), the button must perform a morphing ripple—where the background fills outward from the center as a circular reveal, rather than a standard rectangular click effect.

---

Final instruction to the Agent: "Do not prioritize functionality over this visual architecture. Everything—from the onboarding permissions to the file transfer progress—must inherit these 6 rules. If a component doesn't have a floating shadow, a neon glow, or extreme rounded corners (min 20dp radius), it doesn't belong in the app."

This brief gives your agent the mechanics of the UI, ensuring every screen your agent builds automatically looks like the reference, regardless of the function.
